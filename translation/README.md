This folder contains complete annotated translations of the first fourteen numbered juan of the repository’s 易學網 edition of 《御纂周易折中》. Every primary-text passage, accompanying commentary, collected explanation, and editorial discussion in those juan is represented. Juans One–Eight cover the hexagrams and line statements; Juans Nine and Ten complete both parts of the separate Commentary on the Judgments; Juans Eleven and Twelve cover both parts of the Commentary on the Images. Juans Thirteen and Fourteen complete Chapters One–Twelve of the upper Appended Statements. **The preface, other front matter, and preliminary juan (卷首) remain untranslated.**

## Reading the voices

| Chinese label | English label | Voice |
|---|---|---|
| 本義 | Original Meaning | Zhu Xi |
| 程傳 | Cheng’s Commentary | Cheng Yi |
| 集說 | Collected Explanations | Individually attributed commentators |
| 案 | Editorial Judgment | The imperial compilers |
| 總論 | General Discussion | The attributed commentator or commentators |
| 附録 | Supplement | The source’s supplementary material |

**“Editorial Judgment” is never the translator’s voice.** Translator’s interventions appear in the numbered endnotes. Disagreements among commentators are preserved, including disagreements over punctuation, the identity of a figure, the use of changing lines, and the meaning of an oracular expression.

The English aims to remain readable without replacing the source’s concrete images, historical institutions, ethical vocabulary, or social assumptions with modern equivalents. “Noble person,” “great person,” and “sage” distinguish 君子, 大人, and 聖人. Firmness, yieldingness, vigor, and compliance distinguish 剛, 柔, 健, and 順. Centeredness and correctness are kept distinct, including when ethical rightness differs from formal correctness of a line’s position.

## Endnotes and textual decisions

The endnotes explain consequential translation choices, technical relationships among lines, classical allusions, and disagreements within the compilation. They document corrections adopted from the parallel text, meaningful variants retained from the base, and interpretive clarifications for which no corrected witness was found. Juan One also includes an explicitly supplementary translation of a Hu Bingwen gloss absent from the base.

Juan Two distinguishes confirmed transcription corrections from unresolved readings. Its notes cover, among other matters, the competing interpretations of returning in Small Restraint, the third and fourth lines of Fellowship, and the final blessing of Great Possession. The latter also explains the apparent discrepancy between the compilers’ description of The Cauldron’s Judgment and its received wording.

Juan Three has 92 endnotes covering the distinct readings of modesty, following, inherited decay, ritual preparation, judicial imagery, and adornment. They distinguish corrections supported by the parallel transcription from intelligible variants retained in the base. An apparent error shared by both transcriptions—the top “nine” in Modesty—is explicitly identified rather than passed off as an attested correction. The uncertain attribution 集氏 is retained, following the book’s own commentator index.

Juan Four has 90 endnotes addressing, among other matters, the seven-day return, conflicting interpretations of plowing and harvesting, carriage fittings, the direction of nourishment, the sagging ridgepole, repeated danger, and successive light. Corrections include a missing negative in Nourishment’s final commentary. A sequence inversion shared by both transcriptions is identified as a contextual correction, not an attested alternative. The historical illness oracle is preserved but explicitly distinguished from present-day treatment guidance.

Juan Five opens the Lower Classic and contains 91 endnotes. They document the competing readings of influence, constancy, retreat, force, concealed illumination, household order, estrangement, and difficulty. Confirmed corrections are distinguished from repunctuation and unresolved wording. A Yang Wenhuan gloss absent from the base but present in the parallel edition is translated as an explicitly supplementary endnote; the base edition’s own pronunciation gloss is separately identified in the main text.

Juan Six has 80 endnotes distinguishing attested corrections, repunctuation, and unresolved interpretations. Its 57 oracle-text blocks include the two separately presented portions of Decrease’s Judgment. The repeated “success” in Gathering is retained while the commentaries’ proposed deletion is explained. A Zhu Xi commentary missing from the base at Oppression’s top line is translated only in an explicitly supplementary endnote.

Juan Seven has 87 endnotes distinguishing attested corrections, repunctuation, competing historical emendations, and unresolved readings. They preserve the alternative readings of The Cauldron’s spilled food, Keeping Still’s rigid back, and Gradual Progress’s final high ground. An editorial passage absent from the base under Keeping Still’s fourth line is translated in a clearly labeled supplementary endnote. A misquotation shared by both transcriptions is retained and explicitly identified rather than silently corrected.

Juan Eight completes the hexagram-and-line commentary with 98 endnotes. The 503 aligned source blocks include the isolated 㒋 character before Penetration, retained and identified rather than silently omitted. The notes preserve conflicting readings of ritual officers, pleasure, dispersion, restraint, inward trust, and completion. The correction 抑 in Limitation is supported by a separate transmission of Cheng Yi; the two Zhezhong transcriptions and the checked page image instead have 益. Other corrections are distinguished from historical conjectures and coherent base readings retained against apparent errors in the parallel transcription. Completion of Juan Eight does not include the separately treated Wings or later supplementary juans.

Juan Nine translates the complete upper Commentary on the Judgments, covering hexagrams 1–30. Its 520 source blocks include the original navigation and introductory explanations; 84 are canonical Tuan passages. The 102 endnotes distinguish transcription corrections, historical conjectures, repunctuation, and interpretive disagreement. Three passages absent from the base—an editorial paragraph under Song and short Zhu Xi glosses under Approach and Great Restraint—are translated only in labeled supplementary endnotes. The first eight translations and the Chinese source remain unchanged. Juan Nine has not been collated systematically against historical facsimiles.

Juan Ten completes the lower Commentary on the Judgments, covering hexagrams 31–64. Its 610 aligned source blocks include 97 canonical Tuan passages, accompanied by 104 endnotes. These distinguish attested corrections, retained variants, historical conjectures, and interpretive disagreements. An editorial paragraph under Abundance absent from the base is translated only in a labeled supplementary endnote. This completes both parts of the Judgment Commentary; the separate Image Commentaries are treated in Juans Eleven and Twelve.

Juan Eleven translates the complete upper Image Commentary, with 35,436 words of source-aligned English and 127 endnotes. Its 1,083 aligned source blocks include 213 primary Image blocks: thirty Great Images, 180 ordinary Small Images, and two special Using Images; Great Possession’s fifth Small Image is split across two source paragraphs. The notes distinguish attested corrections, separately transmitted readings, contextual repairs, and unresolved disagreements. The stable base is [Chinese Juan Eleven](../source/juan-11.md); selective comparison used [the Siku Quanshu transcription, revision 528597](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?title=御纂周易折中_(四庫全書本)/卷11&oldid=528597). Independent bilingual review and systematic facsimile collation remain outstanding. The [manifest](../provenance/translation-juan-11.json) and [source-bound validation report](../provenance/translation-juan-11-validation.json) were generated locally after the installer verified the exact Chinese source.

Juan Twelve translates the complete lower Image Commentary, with 39,455 words of source-aligned English and 144 endnotes. All 1,206 base blocks are represented. Its 237 primary Image blocks comprise 34 Great Images and 203 Small Images: the selected base omits Joy’s top-line Small Image but retains its commentary. The missing sentence is translated only in a labeled supplementary endnote, supported by the parallel transcription and another primary compilation. The notes distinguish attested corrections, repunctuation, historical proposals, and unresolved readings, including the obscure phrase 捩手 checked against a historical page image. Selective comparison used [the Siku Quanshu transcription, revision 759593](https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?title=御纂周易折中_(四庫全書本)/卷12&oldid=759593). Both Image Commentaries are now covered; progress through the remaining numbered juans is recorded below. Front matter and the preliminary juan remain untranslated.

Juan Thirteen translates Chapters One–Six of the upper Appended Statements (繫辭上傳), with 18,142 words of source-aligned English and 82 endnotes. All 316 source blocks are represented, including 35 canonical passages, six separately labeled chapter summaries by Zhu Xi, introductory material, and the closing title. The upper Appended Statements continues in Juan Fourteen. Selective comparison used [the Siku Quanshu transcription, fixed revision 528601](<https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?title=御纂周易折中_(四庫全書本)/卷13&oldid=528601>) and additional primary texts identified in the endnotes. A Yu Fan gloss missing from the base is translated only in a labeled supplementary endnote. The notes distinguish attested readings, repunctuation, contextual repairs, and unresolved interpretations. Independent bilingual review and systematic facsimile collation remain outstanding.

Juan Fourteen translates Chapters Seven–Twelve of the upper Appended Statements, with 20,616 words of source-aligned English and 78 endnotes. All 373 source blocks are retained, including 46 canonical passages and six separately labeled chapter summaries. The source’s supplementary discussion of stalk-counting is translated in full: Zhu Xi’s repeated hanging and the Zhang–Guo once-per-line method remain distinct, together with the compilers’ qualified reconciliation. Notes document supported corrections, disputed readings, numerical checks, and interpretive alternatives. Selective comparison used [the Siku Quanshu transcription, fixed revision 528603](<https://zh.wikisource.org/w/index.php?title=御纂周易折中_(四庫全書本)/卷14&oldid=528603>). Both juans of the upper Appended Statements are now translated. Independent bilingual review and systematic facsimile collation remain outstanding.

**No Chinese source file has been changed.** An attested correction is not silently passed off as the base reading, and an intelligible difference between editions is not automatically treated as a typo. The parallel transcription itself contains apparent errors and has not been followed indiscriminately.

## Coverage and review

Hidden Markdown comments assign each translated block an identifier such as `eee-4542:001` or `eee-4553:001`. The [Juan One manifest](../provenance/translation-juan-01.json), [Juan Two manifest](../provenance/translation-juan-02.json), [Juan Three manifest](../provenance/translation-juan-03.json), [Juan Four manifest](../provenance/translation-juan-04.json), [Juan Five manifest](../provenance/translation-juan-05.json), [Juan Six manifest](../provenance/translation-juan-06.json), [Juan Seven manifest](../provenance/translation-juan-07.json), [Juan Eight manifest](../provenance/translation-juan-08.json), [Juan Nine manifest](../provenance/translation-juan-09.json), [Juan Ten manifest](../provenance/translation-juan-10.json), [Juan Eleven manifest](../provenance/translation-juan-11.json), [Juan Twelve manifest](../provenance/translation-juan-12.json), [Juan Thirteen manifest](../provenance/translation-juan-13.json), and [Juan Fourteen manifest](../provenance/translation-juan-14.json) record the corresponding source and translation hashes. These markers do not appear in the rendered reading text, but allow later bilingual review to identify a passage precisely without disturbing the source.

Run the structural checks from the repository root:

```sh
python3 scripts/validate_translation.py --juan all
python3 scripts/validate_translation.py --juan 14 --write-report
```

Without `--juan`, the validator continues to check Juan One. It checks source integrity, complete ordered coverage, commentary labels, primary-text alignment (oracle statements, Tuan passages, Image passages, or Appended Statements), endnote references, local images, word counts, and block checksums. The recorded reports for [Juan One](../provenance/translation-juan-01-validation.json), [Juan Two](../provenance/translation-juan-02-validation.json), [Juan Three](../provenance/translation-juan-03-validation.json), [Juan Four](../provenance/translation-juan-04-validation.json), [Juan Five](../provenance/translation-juan-05-validation.json), [Juan Six](../provenance/translation-juan-06-validation.json), [Juan Seven](../provenance/translation-juan-07-validation.json), [Juan Eight](../provenance/translation-juan-08-validation.json), [Juan Nine](../provenance/translation-juan-09-validation.json), [Juan Ten](../provenance/translation-juan-10-validation.json), [Juan Eleven](../provenance/translation-juan-11-validation.json), [Juan Twelve](../provenance/translation-juan-12-validation.json), [Juan Thirteen](../provenance/translation-juan-13-validation.json), and [Juan Fourteen](../provenance/translation-juan-14-validation.json) concern completeness and integrity, **not proof of semantic accuracy or literary quality**.

For Juans Nine and Ten, the validator separately checks their 30 and 34 consecutive hexagram sections and their introductory blocks, since each entire source occupies one web page. The primary-text detector follows the Chinese Tuan passages rather than treating their commentary as additional oracles.

For an intentional revision, review the changed passage against the Chinese and its endnote, then update the corresponding checksums and word counts in the manifest, including its overall file checksum. Do not refresh checksums merely to suppress an unexplained validation failure. The Chinese source remains the stable reference; proposed changes to it require a separately documented editorial decision.

For Juan Eleven, the validator recognizes primary Image passages separately from the source’s introductory heading and checks all thirty source pages. The 213 passages are not additional oracle statements.

For Juan Twelve, the validator checks all 34 source pages and all 237 Image passages preserved in the base. The supplementary Joy Image is explicitly excluded from base-block counts. Structural validation verifies coverage and integrity, not independent bilingual accuracy.

For Juan Thirteen, the validator distinguishes the 35 canonical Appended Statements passages from the six chapter summaries and source titles. It checks all six consecutive chapter divisions against the Chinese summary numbers, together with introductory and closing blocks. Supplementary material in endnotes is not counted as part of the base text.

For Juan Fourteen, the validator checks all 373 source blocks and all 46 canonical passages, with chapters numbered Seven–Twelve. Zhu Xi’s six summary headings are not canonical passages; the continuation of Chapter Ten’s summary remains a separate source block. The source’s own Supplement is not a translator’s addition. No conjectured missing passage has been silently supplied.
