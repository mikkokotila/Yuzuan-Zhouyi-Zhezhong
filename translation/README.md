This folder contains complete annotated translations of the first ten numbered juan of the repository’s 易學網 edition of 《御纂周易折中》. Every primary-text passage, accompanying commentary, collected explanation, and editorial discussion in those juan is represented. Juans One–Eight cover the hexagrams and line statements; Juans Nine and Ten complete both parts of the separate Commentary on the Judgments. **The preface, other front matter, and preliminary juan (卷首) remain untranslated.**

## Reading the voices

| Chinese label | English label | Voice |
|---|---|---|
| 本義 | Original Meaning | Zhu Xi |
| 程傳 | Cheng’s Commentary | Cheng Yi |
| 集說 | Collected Explanations | Individually attributed commentators |
| 案 | Editorial Judgment | The imperial compilers |
| 總論 | General Discussion | The attributed commentator or commentators |
| 附録 | Supplement | The source’s supplementary material |

**“Editorial Judgment” is never the translator’s voice.** Translator’s interventions appear in the numbered endnotes. Disagreements among commentators are preserved, including disagreements over punctuation, the identity of a figure, the use of changing lines, and the meaning of an oracular expression.

The English aims to remain readable without replacing the source’s concrete images, historical institutions, ethical vocabulary, or social assumptions with modern equivalents. “Noble person,” “great person,” and “sage” distinguish 君子, 大人, and 聖人. Firmness, yieldingness, vigor, and compliance distinguish 剛, 柔, 健, and 順. Centeredness and correctness are kept distinct, including when ethical rightness differs from formal correctness of a line’s position.

## Endnotes and textual decisions

The endnotes explain consequential translation choices, technical relationships among lines, classical allusions, and disagreements within the compilation. They document corrections adopted from the parallel text, meaningful variants retained from the base, and interpretive clarifications for which no corrected witness was found. Juan One also includes an explicitly supplementary translation of a Hu Bingwen gloss absent from the base.

Juan Two distinguishes confirmed transcription corrections from unresolved readings. Its notes cover, among other matters, the competing interpretations of returning in Small Restraint, the third and fourth lines of Fellowship, and the final blessing of Great Possession. The latter also explains the apparent discrepancy between the compilers’ description of The Cauldron’s Judgment and its received wording.

Juan Three has 92 endnotes covering the distinct readings of modesty, following, inherited decay, ritual preparation, judicial imagery, and adornment. They distinguish corrections supported by the parallel transcription from intelligible variants retained in the base. An apparent error shared by both transcriptions—the top “nine” in Modesty—is explicitly identified rather than passed off as an attested correction. The uncertain attribution 集氏 is retained, following the book’s own commentator index.

Juan Four has 90 endnotes addressing, among other matters, the seven-day return, conflicting interpretations of plowing and harvesting, carriage fittings, the direction of nourishment, the sagging ridgepole, repeated danger, and successive light. Corrections include a missing negative in Nourishment’s final commentary. A sequence inversion shared by both transcriptions is identified as a contextual correction, not an attested alternative. The historical illness oracle is preserved but explicitly distinguished from present-day treatment guidance.

Juan Five opens the Lower Classic and contains 91 endnotes. They document the competing readings of influence, constancy, retreat, force, concealed illumination, household order, estrangement, and difficulty. Confirmed corrections are distinguished from repunctuation and unresolved wording. A Yang Wenhuan gloss absent from the base but present in the parallel edition is translated as an explicitly supplementary endnote; the base edition’s own pronunciation gloss is separately identified in the main text.

Juan Six has 80 endnotes distinguishing attested corrections, repunctuation, and unresolved interpretations. Its 57 oracle-text blocks include the two separately presented portions of Decrease’s Judgment. The repeated “success” in Gathering is retained while the commentaries’ proposed deletion is explained. A Zhu Xi commentary missing from the base at Oppression’s top line is translated only in an explicitly supplementary endnote.

Juan Seven has 87 endnotes distinguishing attested corrections, repunctuation, competing historical emendations, and unresolved readings. They preserve the alternative readings of The Cauldron’s spilled food, Keeping Still’s rigid back, and Gradual Progress’s final high ground. An editorial passage absent from the base under Keeping Still’s fourth line is translated in a clearly labeled supplementary endnote. A misquotation shared by both transcriptions is retained and explicitly identified rather than silently corrected.

Juan Eight completes the hexagram-and-line commentary with 98 endnotes. The 503 aligned source blocks include the isolated 㒋 character before Penetration, retained and identified rather than silently omitted. The notes preserve conflicting readings of ritual officers, pleasure, dispersion, restraint, inward trust, and completion. The correction 抑 in Limitation is supported by a separate transmission of Cheng Yi; the two Zhezhong transcriptions and the checked page image instead have 益. Other corrections are distinguished from historical conjectures and coherent base readings retained against apparent errors in the parallel transcription. Completion of Juan Eight does not include the separately treated Wings or later supplementary juans.

Juan Nine translates the complete upper Commentary on the Judgments, covering hexagrams 1–30. Its 520 source blocks include the original navigation and introductory explanations; 84 are canonical Tuan passages. The 102 endnotes distinguish transcription corrections, historical conjectures, repunctuation, and interpretive disagreement. Three passages absent from the base—an editorial paragraph under Song and short Zhu Xi glosses under Approach and Great Restraint—are translated only in labeled supplementary endnotes. The first eight translations and the Chinese source remain unchanged. Juan Nine has not been collated systematically against historical facsimiles.

Juan Ten completes the lower Commentary on the Judgments, covering hexagrams 31–64. Its 610 aligned source blocks include 97 canonical Tuan passages, accompanied by 104 endnotes. These distinguish attested corrections, retained variants, historical conjectures, and interpretive disagreements. An editorial paragraph under Abundance absent from the base is translated only in a labeled supplementary endnote. Both parts of the Judgment Commentary are now translated; the separate Image Commentaries and remaining material still follow.

**No Chinese source file has been changed.** An attested correction is not silently passed off as the base reading, and an intelligible difference between editions is not automatically treated as a typo. The parallel transcription itself contains apparent errors and has not been followed indiscriminately.

## Coverage and review

Hidden Markdown comments assign each translated block an identifier such as `eee-4542:001` or `eee-4553:001`. The [Juan One manifest](../provenance/translation-juan-01.json), [Juan Two manifest](../provenance/translation-juan-02.json), [Juan Three manifest](../provenance/translation-juan-03.json), [Juan Four manifest](../provenance/translation-juan-04.json), [Juan Five manifest](../provenance/translation-juan-05.json), [Juan Six manifest](../provenance/translation-juan-06.json), [Juan Seven manifest](../provenance/translation-juan-07.json), [Juan Eight manifest](../provenance/translation-juan-08.json), [Juan Nine manifest](../provenance/translation-juan-09.json), and [Juan Ten manifest](../provenance/translation-juan-10.json) record the corresponding source and translation hashes. These markers do not appear in the rendered reading text, but allow later bilingual review to identify a passage precisely without disturbing the source.

Run the structural checks from the repository root:

```sh
python3 scripts/validate_translation.py --juan all
python3 scripts/validate_translation.py --juan 10 --write-report
```

Without `--juan`, the validator continues to check Juan One. It checks source integrity, complete ordered coverage, commentary labels, primary-text alignment (oracle statements or Tuan passages), endnote references, local images, word counts, and block checksums. The recorded reports for [Juan One](../provenance/translation-juan-01-validation.json), [Juan Two](../provenance/translation-juan-02-validation.json), [Juan Three](../provenance/translation-juan-03-validation.json), [Juan Four](../provenance/translation-juan-04-validation.json), [Juan Five](../provenance/translation-juan-05-validation.json), [Juan Six](../provenance/translation-juan-06-validation.json), [Juan Seven](../provenance/translation-juan-07-validation.json), [Juan Eight](../provenance/translation-juan-08-validation.json), [Juan Nine](../provenance/translation-juan-09-validation.json), and [Juan Ten](../provenance/translation-juan-10-validation.json) concern completeness and integrity, **not proof of semantic accuracy or literary quality**.

For Juans Nine and Ten, the validator separately checks their 30 and 34 consecutive hexagram sections and their introductory blocks, since each entire source occupies one web page. The primary-text detector follows the Chinese Tuan passages rather than treating their commentary as additional oracles.

For an intentional revision, review the changed passage against the Chinese and its endnote, then update the corresponding checksums and word counts in the manifest, including its overall file checksum. Do not refresh checksums merely to suppress an unexplained validation failure. The Chinese source remains the stable reference; proposed changes to it require a separately documented editorial decision.
